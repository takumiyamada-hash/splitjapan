Tax-free shopping

Japan Tax-Free Shopping Is Changing on November 1, 2026 — Here's Exactly What To Do

By Takumi, in Tokyo · Updated August 2026 · Based on the enacted 2025 tax reform. We'll keep this page current as retailers publish their refund procedures.

If you've shopped tax-free in Japan before, forget what you know. From November 1, 2026, the instant in-store discount is gone. You'll pay the full price including 10% consumption tax at the register, and claim the tax back when you leave Japan — the way it already works in Europe and Korea.

The part nobody tells you: it's not the full 10%

Refunds are processed by the retailer or a refund operator, and major department stores have indicated they will deduct a handling fee of roughly 1.55%–2.2% of the purchase price. On a ¥100,000 haul, expect roughly ¥8,000–¥8,500 back rather than the full ¥9,091 of tax you paid.

Step by step: how to claim your refund

  1. In the store: show your passport at purchase, same as today. Your purchase data is registered electronically (linked to your passport). Keep the receipts anyway.
  2. Before airport check-in: go to the customs verification point — a kiosk, or via Visit Japan Web. Most travelers get a green "go ahead" result; a red result means customs wants to see the goods, so keep tax-free purchases in your carry-on or check them after verification.
  3. Get paid: the refund arrives by the method the retailer offers — typically the card you paid with, cash at an airport counter, or a digital payout. Timing and channel differ by store; this is the part still being finalized by retailers.
Three mistakes to avoid
  • Packing tax-free items deep in checked luggage before customs verification — if you're flagged and can't show the goods, no refund.
  • Leaving no buffer time. The verification step is new for everyone from November 1; expect lines at major airports in the first months.
  • Assuming mailed-home goods count — items shipped overseas from Japan stopped qualifying for tax-free in April 2025.

Is the new system worse?

Mostly no. You lose a little to handling fees and you front the cash during your trip. In exchange: no more sealed bags (use your skincare on day one), no ¥500,000 consumables cap, and — retailers hope — shorter in-store tax-free counter lines, since the paperwork moves to the exit point. The one genuinely new burden is remembering that money is owed to you.
