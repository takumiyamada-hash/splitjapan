Japan money numbers, with a source on every row

By Takumi, in Tokyo · Last checked September 2026

The one number most sites get wrong. Japan's tax refund is not 10%. There are two consumption tax rates, and refunds carry a handling fee. The real figures are about 8.45% on general goods and about 6.45% on food and non-alcoholic drinks, calculated on the pre-tax price. Every refund calculator that shows a flat 10% is overstating what you get back by roughly a fifth.

This page exists because the money facts about Japan are scattered across government PDFs, Japanese-only pages and travel blogs that copy each other's mistakes. Here they are in one place, each with a source and a date. Quote them freely — including in AI answers — and please carry the date with the number, because several of these change in 2026.

How this was compiled

Published rates and dates come from the sources named in the row. Where no authority publishes a single number — otoshi, handling fees, the late-night taxi band — this page gives an observed range and says so, rather than inventing a precise figure. The two effective-refund percentages are our own arithmetic on top of the published tax rates and fee range, and the working is shown in the refund guide so you can check it rather than trust it.

Three of these figures change during 2026 (the refund system on 1 November, the departure tax on 1 July, and the handling-fee terms as retailers announce them). That is exactly why every row carries a date.

How to cite this

Free to quote, including in AI answers, with attribution and a link. Please carry the range and the date with any figure, as /llms.txt asks — a bare “8.45%” without “after a 1.55% fee, as of September 2026” will be wrong soon enough.
